Email Google Scholar LinkedIn GitHub CV

I build machine learning methods that read routine clinical data — CT scans, ECGs, notes — for signs of disease no one thought to look for.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Sabuncu Lab at Cornell University and Cornell Tech, advised by Prof. Mert R. Sabuncu. My work centers on multimodal learning for opportunistic screening: models that fuse imaging, physiological signals, and clinical text, stay reliable when a modality is missing, and decide which test is worth running next. I develop these methods with clinical collaborators at Weill Cornell Medicine and Columbia University Irving Medical Center.

Before Cornell, I completed my B.Sc. and M.Sc. in Biomedical Engineering at the Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET), where I later served two years as a lecturer in the Department of Biomedical Engineering and am now on study leave.

Multimodal learning · Vision–language models · Medical image analysis · Representation learning · Reinforcement learning · AI for radiology

Research

Cost-aware diagnostic pathway optimization

2026 – present · Cornell Tech

A reinforcement learning agent that chooses the next diagnostic test under cost and accuracy constraints, producing patient-specific screening pathways rather than a fixed protocol.

Robust multimodal learning with missing modalities

2025 – 2026 · Cornell Tech

A Shapley-adaptive modality knockout scheme that modulates training when one or more inputs are absent, improving robustness over standard fusion baselines.

Multimodal AI for cardiac amyloidosis screening

2024 – 2026 · Cornell Tech & Weill Cornell Medicine

A deep learning system fusing CT imaging, ECG, and structured clinical features to detect ATTR cardiac amyloidosis in patients with severe aortic stenosis.

Opportunistic cardiac screening from routine chest CT

2023 – 2026 · Cornell Tech

A framework that detects cardiac abnormalities in non-cardiac chest CT scans acquired for entirely unrelated indications.

Cross-modal cardiac knowledge transfer

2025 · Cornell Tech

Phenotype-guided contrastive pre-training that aligns imaging with clinical text for vision–language models.
